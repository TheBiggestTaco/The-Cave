OBSERVATORY

The universe begins at its first tick.

Particles attract, repel at short distances, and form/break spring bonds. Stable connected structures emerge if the rules permit them.

Toy physics, not a cosmological model. Fixed-step seeded simulation; reset repeats the same initial conditions.

Start with five moving atoms · 4 autonomous cyan atoms · 1 player-controlled gold atom. Create more atoms wherever you stand (up to 64 total). Line loops enclose a room only while all four edges are connected. Circles are independent boundaries you can enter and exit. These are small-population versions of laboratory-style forces; the two simulators do not yet share one physics module.

Rule Map

Five-atom play experiment: four autonomous atoms and one player atom. Four connected line edges can form an enclosed room; independent circular boundaries can contain the player. Both arise from geometry rather than a scripted room-completion event. The Play physics are simplified and not yet the exact same implementation as the Big Bang Lab.

Earlier atomic prototype v1: every room point contains the same four potential connection properties: elasticity (teal), resistance (gold), curvature (purple), and closure (rose). Links attach to directional internal ports; mutation changes the atom's rotation and appearance. These are experimental visual expressions, not yet the full laboratory physics.

Points connect into lines. Closing a loop forms a room. Opening a room creates a corridor. Crossing a corridor reveals another chamber.

Future generations may change these rules, while preserving this playable ancestor.
